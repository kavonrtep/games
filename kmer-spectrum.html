What this method does

Count every k-mer (word of length k) in the raw reads – no assembly, no reference – and plot how many different k-mers were seen once, twice, three times… The shape of this k-mer spectrum tells you:

  • the genome size (step 3),
  • the sequencing error rate (step 4),
  • the heterozygosity of a diploid genome (step 5),
  • how repetitive the genome is (step 6).

In the practical: jellyfish count + jellyfish histo, then GenomeScope. Every step here uses a genome and reads simulated in your browser, so the true answer is always known.

Sequencing & counting

k-mers are counted as canonical k-mers (a k-mer and its reverse complement are one), as jellyfish count -C does.

GenomeScope in the browser

A port of GenomeScope 2.0 (Ranallo-Benavidez, Jaron & Schatz 2020, Nat. Commun. 11:1432) – the same model, the same fitting procedure and the same summary as the R tool and genomescope.org. Use it when the website is not available.

  • The k-mer spectrum is modelled as peaks at 1×, 2×, … the k-mer coverage (kcov), each a negative binomial (dup = its extra spread); the fit uses the transformed spectrum n·h(n).
  • Errors are the k-mers on the left that the model does not explain (orange); unique sequence (yellow) is the part of the model from single-copy sequence.
  • Haploid length = all k-mers except errors / (ploidy × kcov). min–max = estimate ± 2 standard errors.

Ploidy 1 and 2 only; for polyploids use the R tool. Small differences from R (well inside its min–max range) can occur when a parameter ends at its limit.

Making the histogram

jellyfish count -C -m 21 -s 50M -t 4 \
  -o reads.jf <(zcat reads_R1.fastq.gz reads_R2.fastq.gz)
jellyfish histo reads.jf > reads.histo

Set the k-mer length to the -m of jellyfish count. Bacteria are haploid (ploidy 1). The last line of jellyfish histo collects all k-mers seen ≥ 10 000 times; GenomeScope leaves it out of the fit.
